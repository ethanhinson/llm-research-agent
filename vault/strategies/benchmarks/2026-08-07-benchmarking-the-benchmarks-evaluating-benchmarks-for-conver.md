# Benchmarking the Benchmarks: Evaluating Benchmarks for Conversational Agents

## Summary
This work addresses a gap in AI evaluation by proposing a framework to assess the quality of benchmarks used for task-oriented conversational agents. The framework uses LLM judges to evaluate benchmark consistency, complexity, and policy coverage—dimensions often overlooked when benchmarks themselves are created.

## How It Works
The framework is reference-free and employs LLM judges to measure three attributes: consistency (tasks are well-formed and coherent), complexity (scenarios have sufficient depth), and policy coverage (the benchmark exercises a range of agent behaviors). The authors validate the approach by comparing LLM judge scores against independent human annotations and by testing on benchmarks generated by LLMs of different capabilities and on benchmarks intentionally degraded via perturbations.

## Why It Matters
Poor benchmarks—those with inconsistent tasks, simplistic scenarios, or narrow coverage—produce unreliable agent evaluations and can mislead development efforts. Having a systematic, reference-free way to audit benchmark quality before relying on it reduces the risk that evaluation results reflect benchmark flaws rather than agent capability. The framework's consistency across domains and judge models makes it a practical diagnostic tool for both synthetic and human-curated benchmarks.
