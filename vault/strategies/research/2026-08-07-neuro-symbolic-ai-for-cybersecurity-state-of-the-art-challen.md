# Neuro-Symbolic AI for Cybersecurity: State of the Art, Challenges, and Opportunities

## Summary
This systematic review analyzes 103 peer-reviewed publications on Neuro-Symbolic (NeSy) AI applied to cybersecurity from 2019–2026, categorizing systems across three integration tiers. The core finding is that combining neural learning with symbolic reasoning addresses fundamental gaps in pure neural (lack of interpretability) and pure symbolic (lack of adaptability) approaches, with deeper integration correlating to measurable performance gains across intrusion detection, malware analysis, and vulnerability discovery.

## How It Works
The authors employed SPAR-4-SLR systematic review methodology, searching eight major databases and specialized venues using combined search terms for neuro-symbolic methods and cybersecurity domains. The 103 selected papers were classified into three tiers: 22 deep-integration systems with end-to-end neural-symbolic coupling, 55 structured-interaction systems with meaningful integration, and 26 contextual baselines. Papers were evaluated using a Grounding-Instructibility-Alignment (G-I-A) analytical framework. Key findings include multi-agent and structured architectures outperforming single-agent systems, causal reasoning enabling proactive defense, and knowledge-guided learning improving data efficiency and explainability.

## Why It Matters
Practitioners should care because the survey documents concrete performance improvements (20–50% gains in autonomous operations) and identifies that integration depth correlates with capability advances across cybersecurity domains. However, significant deployment barriers remain: evaluation standardization is nascent, computational costs constrain real-world deployment, and human-AI collaboration patterns are underexplored. The review also surfaces a critical dual-use concern—autonomous offensive systems already achieve zero-day exploitation at reduced cost—making the establishment of community-driven benchmarks, responsible development practices, and defensive alignment urgent priorities for responsible advancement of the field.
