# From Economic Agents to Agentic Economies: A Systems Blueprint for Economic World Models

## Summary
Economic World Models (EWMs) are generative simulations that model how economies evolve by representing heterogeneous agents, their beliefs, actions, and interactions through market and institutional mechanisms. This paper proposes a six-level capability ladder for building EWMs—from rule-based agents to LLM-based agents, self-evolving agents, evolving institutions, and sim-to-real economic twins—and identifies a gap where most existing work remains at lower levels while advanced self-evolving and institutionally endogenous systems remain rare.

## How It Works
The paper organizes EWM development into six capability levels: fixed rule-based agent worlds, adaptive agent worlds, LLM-based agent worlds, self-evolving agents, evolving institutional worlds, and sim-to-real economic twins with empirical alignment. A systematic literature survey maps existing research across these levels to identify development gaps. The authors frame EWMs as generative engines where heterogeneous agents adapt and co-evolve with markets and institutions to produce aggregate economic dynamics from first principles.

## Why It Matters
Practitioners building AI economic simulators and those designing evaluation sandboxes for economic policy or agent training need a structured roadmap to move beyond compartmentalized agent models toward systems with self-adapting agents and endogenous institutions. The capability ladder and literature survey provide a blueprint for identifying which components remain underdeveloped, helping guide resource allocation toward high-fidelity economic simulation environments that can serve as both decision-support tools for humans and safer training substrates for autonomous agents operating in economic contexts.
