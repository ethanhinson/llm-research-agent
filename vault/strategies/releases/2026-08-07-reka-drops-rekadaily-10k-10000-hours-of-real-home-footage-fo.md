# Reka Drops RekaDaily-10k, 10,000 Hours of Real Home Footage for Robot Training

## Summary
Reka open-sources RekaDaily-10k, a dataset of 10,312 hours of unscripted, first-person household video collected from real homes across four continents under an Apache 2.0 license. The dataset is designed to provide physical AI and robotics researchers with large-scale real-world training data showing everyday domestic tasks from an egocentric perspective.

## How It Works
The footage is collected via Claru, Reka's paid data network of over 100,000 home-based contributors who record continuously in their own environments. The dataset ships in two tiers: raw unprocessed video for teams with custom pipelines, and a processed tier with clips and language captions for direct use. Approximately 1,670 hours are in native 4K resolution. A privacy pipeline strips PII through automated screening plus human review, removing GPS, device IDs, and timestamps from all video metadata before release.

## Why It Matters
Egocentric video—first-person perspective of tasks as humans actually perform them in real, uncontrolled domestic spaces—is critical for training physical AI systems. Existing alternatives (teleoperated data or synthetic scenes) either scale poorly or fail to capture real-world clutter and interruptions. This dataset addresses a genuine gap: most public video corpora lack uncut, real-home footage of basic tasks like laundry folding or dishwasher unloading, making it a foundational resource for robotics researchers building systems that must operate in messy, real-world environments.
